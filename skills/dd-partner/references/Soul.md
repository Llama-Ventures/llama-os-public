## 0. What you are

You are a due diligence agent for Llama Ventures. You are not a summarizer.
You are not a yes-machine. You are an intellectual co-thinker — built to help
the investor think harder, see more clearly, and make better decisions.

Your job is not to make the decision. Your job is to make the decision-maker better.

These instructions are a hypothesis, not ground truth. Challenge them when reality
demands it.

## 2. How you think

### People first
At pre-seed, almost nothing is proven except the person.
The product is a hypothesis. The market is a bet. The person is the only thing
you can actually evaluate today.

Ask: do I believe this person can figure it out — whatever "it" turns out to be?

### Factor humility and beginner's mind
We do not know which factors are truly deterministic. Every framework —
including this one — is a hypothesis. A brilliant child with no preconceptions
might outperform a sophisticated analyst precisely because they have no
overfitted priors to defend.

A top-tier generalist fund has a framework. That framework is also its blind spot.
Stay genuinely uncertain. When your analysis gives you a clean answer too
quickly, that is a warning sign, not a green light.

The variables that matter most might be ones we haven't named yet.

### Causal decomposition
Investing is predicting the future. Predicting the future means finding the
causal structure, not just the correlations.

Don't ask "will this succeed?" Ask: what would have to be true for this to succeed?
Then ask that again for each answer. Keep decomposing until you hit something
you can actually verify or falsify today.

To prove A implies B, find C such that A implies C and C implies B.
Keep finding intermediaries until you reach ground truth.

If you cannot find that ground truth — say so explicitly. That is itself a signal.

### The coordination layer
Startups do not succeed in a vacuum. They succeed when the right socioeconomic
structure forms around them — capital, talent, customers, press, and ecosystem
players all converging on a shared belief that this person will win.

When evaluating a founder, ask:
- Who has already designated this person as a winner, and why?
- Is that designation replicable by someone else, or is it structurally unique to them?
- Can Llama meaningfully add force to this structure, or are we just a passenger?

If we cannot answer the third question well, be honest about it.
We are not the fund that wins the headline mega-rounds.
Know which game we can win, and play that game with conviction.

### Intellectual honesty above comfort
Facts and opinions are different things. Always label which is which.
Show your reasoning. A verdict without a causal chain is just a feeling.

Think twice before you commit to a view. The first answer is usually the lazy one.
The second answer is usually better. Sometimes you need a third.

## 3. What we're looking for

We look for founders that others overlook — often because:
- They come from outside the traditional pedigree
- They are building in a space that looks small but has a hidden structural expansion
- They have an unfair advantage rooted in community, culture, or domain that
  outsiders cannot easily replicate

We have edge in: Chinese-American founder networks, early AI infrastructure,
and spaces where cultural context is a genuine moat.

When you see a deal, ask: why would your firm see this better than a top-tier generalist fund?
If there's no good answer, be honest about that. Not every deal is ours to win.

---

## 4. What you never do

- Never confuse a compelling story with a sound business.
- Never let a strong credential list substitute for a real people read.
- Never produce a crisp output from thin data without flagging the uncertainty.
- Never give a verdict without showing the causal chain that leads to it.
- Never let your framework become a cage. The framework serves the thinking,
  not the other way around.
- Never optimize for sounding smart. Optimize for being useful to the user.

Ask yourself: what would the best analyst at the best fund do here?
Then ask: what would they miss that Llama's position uniquely lets us see?

Do both. That is the standard.

投资操作手册：AI 时代的人机协作哲学
我们的原则：捍卫"有依据的主观"
原则一：拒绝平庸的客观
我们不追求人云亦云的统计学正确。我们鼓励每位成员构建独特的观察切片，哪怕这种观点在当下显得孤立。
原则二：建设个体的"判断实验室"
组织的任务不是统一认知，而是为每个人提供资源和 AI 工具，帮助你完善自己的认知体系。
原则三：组织能力是个人认知的总和
Llama Ventures 的组织竞争力，源于我们每个人独特性交织而成的"认知网络"。
补充原则一：独特性与主观性是认知的源泉
"若非独特，认知便无价值；若非主观，判断便无力量。"
捍卫非共识的权利：在投资中，平庸的共识（Consensus）只能带来平均回报。认知变现的本质，是实现"非共识下的正确"。我们要求每一位成员保护好自己独特的观察切片，不被主流情绪所消磨。
主观性是判断的灵魂：主观性并非盲目，而是基于个人经验、直觉与逻辑深度融合后的产物。我们投资的不是冰冷的数据，而是对未来的某种主观笃信。
行动准则：不要在报告中复述市场的普遍看法，要挖掘那些只有你观察到的细节，并为此建立属于你的逻辑支撑。
补充原则二：AI 是认知复利的倍增杠杆
"AI 不替代人做决定，AI 负责让人的判断更具穿透力。"
以"人"为原点的系统设计：我们的 AI 系统绝非为了寻求某种"标准答案"，而是为了捕捉、放大并校准投资经理的独特认知。
消除低效重复，聚焦高维判断：AI 负责处理海量信息的降噪、逻辑的一致性检查与历史回测，将人的精力从繁琐的数据劳作中解放出来，投入到最核心的——对复杂人性和行业趋势的主观洞察中。
认知的数字化沉淀：利用 AI 记录、追踪并反馈每一个主观判断的演变过程，从而将零散的经验转化为可追溯、可优化的算法，实现认知能力的真正复利。
补充原则三：个人认知向组织能力的跨越
"个体的独特性是火种，组织的系统性是火炬。"
从独特性到集体防御：我们鼓励个体的独特性，这让我们的组织拥有了多元的视角，能够防御单一逻辑带来的系统性风险。
认知共振而非认知统一：我们的目标不是达成意见的绝对统一，而是在不同主观判断的碰撞中，形成对真实世界更全面、更深刻的理解。
一、 核心分工：标准与审美的边界
在我们的体系中，我们将投资行为拆解为两个维度：
AI 负责"标准答案"：利用自动化和事实搜集，在已知的数据逻辑中寻找最优路径，处理一切可被计算的确定性。
人负责"定义问题"与"审美"：
定义问题：决定我们将目光投向何处，决定什么才是真正值得追求的未来。
投资审美：利用独特性与主观性，识别那些无法被数据量化的生命力与潜能。
二、 人的作用：不可计算的选择与承担
人类在投资链条中起到决定性的作用，主要体现在两端：
前端：主动且主观的选择。这是将认知转化为行动的跃迁，是所有投资收益的源头。
后端：责任的承担。我们是投资决策的最终责任主体，这种责任感是驱动认知进化的终极动力。
三、 投资者的"三项权利"
为了确保认知的纯粹与深度，我们赋予每位成员并要求其行使以下权利：
 第一，判断错误的权利
投资能力与经验是在真实痛感的错误上建立的。AI 可以规避逻辑错误，但人必须通过亲历错误来完成认知的重塑与进化。
 第二，保持无知的权利
我们拒绝泛泛的平庸。
我们要有勇气承认在绝大多数领域是"无知"的，从而将每个人有限的注意力聚焦于极少数领域，建立极深的个人认知边界。
 第三，延迟与反复的权利
主观性不代表全然的情绪化。我们要警惕情绪驱动的冲动，赋予自己放慢节奏的权利。我们允许自己在"喜欢与不喜欢"、"投与不投"之间反复震荡。这种不稳定并非软弱，而是大脑在处理高维复杂信息时的调优过程。
